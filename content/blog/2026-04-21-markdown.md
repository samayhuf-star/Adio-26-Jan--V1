## Why Click Fraud Hurts B2B SaaS More Than You Think

For ecommerce, click fraud is a direct loss per click. For B2B SaaS, the damage compounds:

- **Higher CPCs + longer sales cycles**: One bad week of fraudulent clicks can inflate CAC across an entire month.
- **Signal pollution**: Smart Bidding learns from click and conversion patterns. Fraudulent clicks create misleading signals that degrade algorithmic optimization.
- **False funnel metrics**: MQL rate, demo-book rate, and conversion paths become unreliable.
- **Territory and competitor distortion**: If you sell to specific geos or industries, repeated invalid clicks can be concentrated and harder to spot.

Google does filter “Invalid Traffic,” but **many paid clicks still pass through**—especially low-grade fraud that looks human enough to avoid automated detection.

## The Most Common Types of Click Fraud on Google Ads (What to Watch For)

Click fraud isn’t always dramatic. In B2B SaaS, it often looks like “normal” activity until you connect the dots.

### 1) Competitor & Vendor Clicks
Repeated clicks from companies that are researching your offer (or trying to drain budget). Often clustered around your brand terms.

### 2) Bot Traffic Mimicking Humans
Modern bots can rotate user agents, locations, and timing patterns to appear natural. They inflate CTR and spend without real intent.

### 3) Click Farms
Human-driven clicks from low-cost labor markets. They’ll often show:
- short session duration
- no meaningful engagement
- repeated patterns across campaigns

### 4) Publisher/Placement Fraud (Display/YouTube)
If you run Display or Performance Max, low-quality placements can generate large volumes of junk clicks.

## How to Identify Click Fraud in Your Google Ads Account

You don’t need a PhD in analytics. You need a consistent monitoring checklist.

### Key red flags (fast diagnostics)
- **High CTR + low conversion rate**, especially on brand campaigns
- **Spike in clicks without a matching spike in impressions**
- **Abnormally high click volume from one geo** you don’t sell to
- **Many clicks from the same IP range** (when you can see it)
- **Short session duration + high bounce rate** on paid landing pages
- **Repeated clicks within minutes** on the same ad group/keyword

### Where to look in Google Ads
- **Campaigns → Locations**: Watch for “suspicious” regions driving spend without pipeline.
- **Auction Insights**: If you see aggressive competitor overlap and unusual behavior on brand terms, investigate.
- **Segment by time**: Fraud often clusters at odd hours or in bursts.

### Where to look outside Google Ads
- **GA4**: Engagement rate and session quality by source/medium and location.
- **CRM (HubSpot/Salesforce)**: Are leads from paid search turning into real accounts, or disappearing?

## How to Block Click Fraud on Google Ads: A Step-by-Step Playbook

Blocking click fraud is most effective when you combine **platform controls** with **behavior-based detection**.

### Step 1: Tighten Geo & Schedule Targeting
Start with the easiest wins:
- Exclude countries/regions you don’t sell to.
- Use **presence** targeting (people *in* your targeted locations), not interest.
- Adjust ad schedule to focus on hours that historically produce qualified leads.

This alone can cut a big chunk of low-intent traffic.

![gif: Excluding non-selling locations and switching location targeting to “Presence” in Google Ads](gif-placeholder)

### Step 2: Build Smarter Segmentation (So Fraud Stands Out)
Fraud hides in blended data. Make it visible:
- Separate **Brand vs Non-Brand** campaigns.
- Split high-intent terms (e.g., “software,” “platform,” “pricing,” “demo”) from top-of-funnel.
- Break out geos if you sell in multiple regions.

When campaigns are segmented, anomalies become obvious—and easier to block.

### Step 3: Use IP Exclusions (But Don’t Rely on Them Alone)
Google Ads allows **IP exclusions** (mainly effective for Search and some campaign types). Use them to block:
- repeat offenders
- known competitor IPs (when confirmed)
- internal traffic (your team, agencies)

Limitations:
- Fraudsters rotate IPs.
- Some traffic comes via mobile networks/proxies.
- You need a reliable way to identify bad IPs.

**IP blocking is necessary, but not sufficient.**

### Step 4: Add Click-Fraud-Aware Negative Keywords
This doesn’t stop bots, but it stops irrelevant intent that looks like fraud:
- “jobs,” “careers,” “internship”
- “free,” “crack,” “torrent”
- “template,” “examples” (depending on your offer)
- competitor terms (if you don’t want the battle)

This reduces junk clicks and improves lead quality.

> Internal linking opportunity: Link to an adiology.io post like `/blog/negative-keywords-b2b-saas` or a downloadable checklist.

### Step 5: Harden Your Landing Pages Against Low-Quality Clickers
Your landing page can help filter and detect:
- Add a **lightweight qualification step** (role/company size/use case).
- Use **bot protection** on forms (reCAPTCHA/honeypot).
- Track micro-conversions (scroll depth, time on page, key button clicks).

If a “click” never engages, treat it as a stronger fraud signal.

### Step 6: Monitor Placement Quality (Especially for PMax/Display)
If you run Display/YouTube/PMax:
- Review placement reports (where available).
- Exclude low-quality apps/sites.
- Consider tighter audience targeting and creative alignment.
- Watch for sudden spikes in clicks from placements that never convert.
