---# Google Keyword Planner: The SaaS Marketer’s No-BS Guide to Finding Keywords That Actually Convert

If you’re using **Google Keyword Planner** like a “keyword idea generator,” you’re leaving money on the table.

Keyword Planner is not built to help you “rank on Google.” It’s built to help advertisers spend. That’s exactly why it’s valuable for SaaS marketers: it reveals **commercial intent** and **budget reality**—two things most SEO tools distort with pretty charts.

This guide shows you how SaaS teams should use Google Keyword Planner to find keywords that drive pipeline (not vanity traffic), how to turn those keywords into a content plan, and how to avoid the most common traps.

---

## What Is Google Keyword Planner (and Why SaaS Teams Should Care)?

**Google Keyword Planner** is a free tool inside Google Ads that provides:

- Keyword ideas Google believes are relevant  
- **Search volume ranges** (more precise if you run ads)  
- **Competition level** (advertiser density, not SEO difficulty)  
- **Bid estimates** (a proxy for commercial value)

For SaaS, that last part is the cheat code: **keywords with high bids usually signal business intent**, meaning the searcher is closer to buying or researching a solution.

**Contrarian truth:** For TOFU content, you still want *some* intent—otherwise you’re funding a blog that educates the market for your competitors.

---

## The 80/20 Setup: How to Access Google Keyword Planner

1. Create a Google Ads account (you don’t need to run ads immediately).
2. In Google Ads: **Tools & Settings → Planning → Keyword Planner**
3. Choose:
   - **Discover new keywords** (for expansion)
   - **Get search volume and forecasts** (for validation)

**Tip:** If Google forces you into campaign setup, create a draft campaign and pause it. You just need access to the tool.

---

## How Google Keyword Planner Actually Works (So You Don’t Misread the Data)

Before you trust any number, understand what it’s telling you:

### 1) “Competition” is NOT SEO competition  
It’s how many advertisers bid on that keyword. For SaaS, that’s useful—because it means companies are paying to acquire those clicks.

### 2) Volume is often a range  
Unless you spend consistently, Keyword Planner may show broad buckets (e.g., 100–1K). That’s fine for TOFU planning—your goal is prioritization, not precision.

### 3) Keyword Planner groups “close variants”  
It will blend similar searches together. Don’t assume it’s one exact query.

**What to do instead:** use it to identify clusters and intent patterns, then build pages/posts around those themes.

## The SaaS Use Case: Find Keywords That Lead to Trials (Not Just Traffic)

Most TOFU content fails because it targets keywords like:

- “what is X”
- “benefits of Y”
- “how to do Z”

…without connecting them to a product-shaped next step.

Here’s the better approach for SaaS: target TOFU keywords that naturally introduce a **workflow**, **category**, or **problem** your product solves.

Examples:
- “how to track marketing performance” (workflow)
- “best SaaS marketing metrics” (category education)
- “how to reduce CAC” (problem)

These won’t convert like BOFU keywords, but they *can* create qualified awareness—and that’s what TOFU should do.

## Step-by-Step: Use Google Keyword Planner to Build a TOFU Keyword Map

### Step 1: Start with “seed” keywords that describe the pain, not your product  
In **Discover new keywords**, enter 5–10 seeds like:

- “reduce churn”
- “SaaS onboarding”
- “product-led growth”
- “CAC payback”
- “SaaS attribution”
- “marketing reporting”

**Why this works:** People don’t wake up searching for your product name. They search for the outcome.

---

### Step 2: Set targeting like a grown-up (or your data will lie)  
In Keyword Planner, set:

- **Location:** your actual market (US-only vs global changes volume massively)  
- **Language:** match your site  
- **Search networks:** Google (not “Google + partners” for cleaner intent)  
- **Date range:** last 12 months to smooth seasonality  

**SaaS gotcha:** If you sell to US + UK + CA + AU, build separate views. Keyword intent differs by region.

---

### Step 3: Filter for commercial value using bid estimates  
In results, add filters for:
- **Top of page bid (low range)** above a threshold (start with $3–$5; adjust by category)
- Exclude irrelevant terms (“free,” “template,” “PDF”) if your ICP doesn’t convert from those

**Why bids matter:** In SaaS, high bids usually correlate with high LTV categories and buyer intent—even for TOFU queries.

---

### Step 4: Create 4 keyword buckets (this is where most teams win or lose)  
Export keywords and group them into:

1. **Problem-aware TOFU:** “why is my CAC increasing”  
2. **Solution-aware TOFU:** “how to improve SaaS retention”  
3. **Category-aware MOFU:** “best marketing analytics tools”  
4. **Competitor/comparison BOFU:** “X vs Y”  

Even if you’re only producing TOFU right now, this framework prevents random content that never ladders into revenue.

---

### Step 5: Pick TOFU topics using the “Intent + Fit” score (simple and brutal)
For each keyword cluster, rate:

- **Intent strength (1–5):** does it imply a business goal?  
- **Product fit (1–5):** can your product logically help?  
- **Content advantage (1–5):** can you say something better than existing SERPs?

Multiply the scores. Only prioritize topics scoring **40+ out of 75**.

**This is the fastest way to stop publishing “nice” articles nobody buys from.**

## The Best Google Keyword Planner Strategies for SaaS Marketers

### Strategy 1: Use Keyword Planner to find “hidden BOFU inside TOFU”
Look for TOFU queries that include:
- “software”
- “tool”
- “platform”
- “best”
- “alternative”
- “reporting”
- “dashboard”
- “analytics”

Example TOFU that quietly converts:
- “marketing performance dashboard”
- “SaaS metrics dashboard”
- “attribution reporting for SaaS”

They look educational, but the reader is already thinking: *I might need a tool.*

---

### Strategy 2: Mine competitor positioning terms (not just competitor names)
Instead of only targeting “HubSpot alternative,” use Keyword Planner to find the words competitors trained the market to search:

- “multi-touch attribution”
- “pipeline velocity”
- “marketing sourced revenue”
- “incrementality testing”

These are “category phrases” that attract high-LTV SaaS buyers.

---

### Strategy 3: Build TOFU clusters around buyer questions sales keeps hearing
Google Keyword Planner is best when paired with internal reality.

Take 10 questions your sales team hears weekly, and turn them into seeds:
- “How do I prove marketing ROI?”
- “What metrics should a SaaS CEO care about?”
- “How do I attribute revenue to campaigns?”

Run these through Keyword Planner, then create topic clusters that match actual objections.

## Common Google Keyword Planner Mistakes (That Waste Months)

### Mistake 1: Chasing volume instead of relevance  
A 20K volume keyword that attracts students > a 300 volume keyword that attracts buyers.

**SaaS content is not a media business.** Your job is qualified awareness that turns into pipeline.

### Mistake 2: Treating Keyword Planner as your only source  
Keyword Planner is advertiser-driven. Pair it with:
- Search Console queries
- CRM closed-won notes
- Customer interviews
- Website on-site search logs

### Mistake 3: Writing one-off posts with no path to product  
TOFU should “teach the problem” *and* introduce the category your product fits into—without turning into a sales page.

If your TOFU posts can’t naturally link to a product page or a demo/trial workflow, they’re probably content theater.

Each post should:
- Define the problem clearly

- Include a lightweight “next step” CTA (not a hard demo push)

## The TOFU CTA That Converts Without Being Pushy

TOFU readers rarely want a demo. They want clarity and a shortcut.

Here’s a CTA structure that works for SaaS:

1) Offer a **practical asset** (checklist, template, teardown)  
2) Tie it to the workflow your product supports  
3) Introduce the product as the “system” that operationalizes it
